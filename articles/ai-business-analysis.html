How AI Is Changing Business Analysis, and What Stays Human

ALZ Technology22 Oct 20262 min read

Artificial intelligence is not replacing business analysis; it is changing where analysts spend their time. Less effort goes into formatting documents and writing first drafts, and more into judgement, validation and stakeholder alignment. In IIBA's Global State of Business Analysis 2026 survey, 69% of professionals said AI has had a positive impact on their careers, with only 5% negative.

Requirements: faster first drafts

  • Turning raw input into structure. AI can turn workshop notes, call transcripts and emails into draft user stories, business rules and open questions.
  • Acceptance criteria. Given a user story or workflow, AI can propose Gherkin-style "Given / When / Then" scenarios as a starting point.
  • Gap and consistency checks. Asking AI to find missing edge cases, conflicting rules or vague words such as "fast" or "user-friendly" is a quick quality pass.
  • Rapid prototypes. Quick mock-ups help stakeholders react to something concrete, surfacing requirement decisions earlier.

UAT: broader coverage, less grunt work

  • Drafting test scenarios and test data from requirements and process maps.
  • Building traceability between requirements, test cases and defects.
  • Summarising defect logs and spotting patterns, such as one screen or rule causing repeated failures.
  • Testing AI-powered features themselves, which needs acceptance thresholds rather than a single "correct" answer.

Data: from question to insight

AI assistants can help write SQL queries, explain unfamiliar datasets, profile data quality and draft dashboard commentary. Analysts still need to confirm the numbers reconcile with the source of truth before anyone makes a decision on them.

The risks to manage

  • Hallucination. AI output can be confident and wrong. Research on AI-assisted requirements analysis consistently stresses strict human review.
  • Confidentiality. Never paste client data, personal data or confidential documents into tools your organisation has not approved.
  • False completeness. A neat document is not a correct one. Missing context does not show up as an error.

What stays human

  • Stakeholder trust and negotiation. Reading the room, resolving conflict and earning buy-in.
  • Understanding the real problem. Asking "why" until the true need emerges behind the stated request.
  • Judgement and accountability. Deciding which trade-offs are acceptable and owning sign-off.
  • Designing human checkpoints. As AI agents enter workflows, IIBA highlights the analyst's role in deciding where people review, intervene and escalate.

The takeaway

Use AI as a fast, tireless junior analyst: let it draft, then you verify, challenge and decide. The value of business analysis is shifting from producing documents to ensuring the right outcome.
